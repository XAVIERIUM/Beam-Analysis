How to use this website

    

      Hi! This little website solves continuous beams the way we do it in Structural Theory class —
      just tell it what your beam looks like, and it will do the tedious arithmetic for you. Here's how:
    

    
    
      
  1. Describe the beam. Put in the total length L, the modulus E (kN/m²)
          and the moment of inertia I (mm⁴). If your beam changes section from span to span — which matters
          a lot in the real calculations — tick “Vary E / I per span” and a small table appears
          where you can type E and I for every span.
    2. 
      
  2. Place the supports. Press + Add support for each one: type its
          position x and pick fixed, pinned, roller or spring (springs need a stiffness k). Settlements go in the
          next column, in metres. Click the little circle at the left of a row to select it, then
          - Delete selected removes it.
    3. 
      
  3. Add the loads. UDL, linearly varying (UVL), point load, point moment or
          horizontal load — with the direction you want. Distributed loads need a start a and an end b.
    4. 
      
  4. Press CALCULATE. On the right you get the support reactions and the four
          pictures: the beam itself, the shear diagram, the bending moment diagram and the deflection curve,
          each with its maximum value marked.
    5. 
      
  5. Want the full working? Choose a method — Three-Moment Equation,
          Slope-Deflection, Moment Distribution or Force Method — and press VIEW SOLUTION. When
          Force Method is chosen, you can also pick how deflections are computed:
          Double Integration, Moment-Area, Virtual Work or Conjugate Beam. That calculation
          appears directly in the Results card and in the full step-by-step solution, and Download .txt saves it
          for your notes or submission.
    6. 
      
  6. Start over with CLEAR ALL whenever you like.
    7.

    Numerical engine: built-in direct-stiffness (matrix) beam solver — a JavaScript port of
    solveBeamFE from MATLABAlamisApp.m (R2025b App Designer app), with the step-by-step
    engines of all four classical methods ported alongside it.
    Sign rules: reactions up positive, moments counterclockwise positive, sagging bending moment positive,
    deflection positive up.
